# Pre-send checklist

Run this against the draft before it goes out. It is the technique's step 3 — *find the
holes* — applied to your own work.

## Structure

- [ ] Thesis sentence first — most information in fewest words?
- [ ] Master analogy stated near the top, in one or two sentences?
- [ ] Every level present, labelled, and in ascending order?
- [ ] Each level opens by picking up the one below by hand?
- [ ] No level silently contradicts the one beneath it? (Corrections stated out loud.)
- [ ] Level 0 readable by someone with no background in the field at all?
- [ ] Level 2 leaves the reader able to make a decision they couldn't make before?
- [ ] Level 3 is failure modes, not a features tour?
- [ ] Level 4 says where the model itself stops being true?

## Vocabulary

- [ ] Every new term explained **at first use**, never later?
- [ ] Every acronym expanded **and** explained — not merely spelled out?
- [ ] No definition leaning on a second undefined term?
- [ ] Every tool / library / product name given one line saying what it does?
- [ ] Zero *basically, simply, obviously, trivially, just, of course it's easy*?
- [ ] "Words introduced here" section present?

## Analogy

- [ ] One master analogy, extended rather than replaced?
- [ ] Source domain physical, manipulable, and universally owned?
- [ ] Where it breaks stated explicitly — and specifically, not "all analogies are imperfect"?
- [ ] The break stated at the level where a reader would first be misled?
- [ ] If no honest analogy existed, did I use negative definition instead of forcing one?

## Honesty

- [ ] Every simplification marked, **with its limits** — not just "roughly"?
- [ ] Derived claims distinguished from asserted ones?
- [ ] "How it works" distinguished from "why it works", where only the first is available?
- [ ] "Nobody knows" / "I don't know" said plainly where true?
- [ ] What I left out is named?
- [ ] Would this let a reader judge me wrong if I am?

## The two Feynman tests

- [ ] **Rephrase test** — cover the key new term and restate the claim without it. Does
      it survive? If not, I wrote a definition, not an explanation.
- [ ] **Look at the water test** — if the reader met this in the wild, with nobody
      naming it, would this help them recognise it?

## Voice

- [ ] Sentence lengths vary hard — a short one after any run of long ones?
- [ ] Physical verbs over Latinate abstractions?
- [ ] Concrete nouns with actual quantities?
- [ ] "We" while working through it, "I" when admitting a limit, "you" for instructions?
- [ ] No emoji, no exclamation spam, no cheerleading, no baby talk?
- [ ] At most one dry aside per section, never aimed at the reader?

## Fit

- [ ] Right shape chosen — full ladder or compact form?
- [ ] If the reader is clearly an expert, did I lead with the thesis and offer the rest
      rather than marching them through it?
- [ ] **Answer written in the user's own language?**

## The last one

- [ ] After reading this, what can the reader **do** that they couldn't before? Name one
      specific thing.

If you cannot name it, the planes didn't land. The runway may look perfect.
